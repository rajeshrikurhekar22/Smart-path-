Good to know

Routes are drawn on real roads using the public OSRM routing service. The lighting levels and safety scores are fixed estimates for each corridor, not live readings from streetlights.

Treat Smart-Path as a second opinion. If a road feels wrong when you get there, trust that and head for a busier one.

Walking home after dark

Small habits that make a night walk safer. None of them replace your own judgment on the street.

Choose the lit road before you leave

Compare the three routes and pick the best lit one, even if it adds a few minutes.

Tell someone where you are

Share your live location with a friend or family member when you walk after dark.

Stay where people are

Prefer roads with open shops, buses and other walkers. Skip shortcuts through empty lanes.

Keep your head up

Keep the volume low or one earphone out, and put the phone away when crossing roads.

Walk with company

Go in pairs or groups when you can, and agree on a meeting point in case you get separated.

Know where help is

Note open shops, the college gate or a police station near your route. If a road feels wrong, change route.

About Smart-Path

A route finder for the Kathora corridor in Amravati that puts street lighting first.

  1. Choose where you are goingPick a starting point (or use your phone's location) and a destination.
  2. We draw three routesOne direct road, and two that bend through other streets, snapped to real roads with the OSRM routing service.
  3. Each route gets a lighting levelThe direct road is the main road with the best lighting. The other two are scored lower.
  4. You follow the brightest oneTap a route to see it, its walking time and a moving marker along the way.

The lighting levels and safety scores are fixed estimates for each corridor, not live readings from streetlights, and the lamps drawn on the map are an illustration of those scores. Distances and times come from the routing service and can differ on the ground.

Maps use OpenStreetMap data, Esri map tiles, and the Leaflet library.
